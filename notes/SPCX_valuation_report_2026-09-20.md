# SPCX valuation report: career-equity decision

**As of September 20, 2026**
**Security:** Space Exploration Technologies Corp. Class A common stock (Nasdaq: SPCX)
**Decision context:** engineer considering a Cursor offer with approximately $8.5 million of face-value SPCX RSUs over four years
**Reference price:** $152.71, the September 18 close

This is an analyst synthesis for decision support, not individualized investment, legal, or tax advice. Dollar amounts are USD. “Market cap” values use the 13.573 billion pro forma basic share count explained below; “EV” means enterprise value. Scenario prices, probabilities, and expected values are this report’s estimates unless explicitly attributed.

## 1. Executive summary

### Direct answer to the “50% lower in two years” concern

A 50% decline from $152.71 means **$76.36 or less**. That outcome is **plausible, not the base case**. This report assigns a **20% central probability** that SPCX closes at or below that threshold in 24 months, with a deliberately wide **15%–30% reasonable range**. This is a subjective endpoint estimate, not an option-implied probability.

The downside has credible anchors:

- Morgan Stanley’s published bear case is **$75**.
- Morningstar’s probability-weighted fair value is **$63**.
- SPCX already fell **53.5%**, from $225.64 to $104.83, in less than two months after the IPO.
- At $152.71, the stock is valued at about **90× trailing sales, 341× trailing EBITDA, and 46× 2026 consensus sales**. Current results must compound extraordinarily quickly to support those multiples.
- More insider stock becomes eligible to trade, cash burn is substantial, and Morgan Stanley’s long-range model requires major external financing.

The offsetting facts are also unusually strong:

- Q2 revenue rose **92% year over year to $7.814 billion** and adjusted EBITDA rose **191% to $3.538 billion**.
- Connectivity is already a real profit engine: **$4.291 billion** of Q2 revenue, **$1.656 billion** of segment operating income, and 12.0 million Starlink subscribers.
- The company reported about **$100.0 billion of cash and marketable securities** against roughly $39.7 billion of debt.
- SpaceX has dominant launch economics, a scaled satellite network, and an integrated AI/compute strategy that could create businesses not represented in trailing financials.
- The tracked Wall Street median 12-month target is **$220**; Morgan Stanley’s base target is **$300**.

The career conclusion is asymmetric:

1. **Do not treat $8.5 million as money already earned.** At $75, the same nominal units would be worth about $4.17 million before vesting, tax, and forfeiture risk.
2. **The role is attractive if the cash compensation and career value still work under a $63–$75 stock case.** The equity then supplies substantial upside rather than being required to make the decision rational.
3. **The principal personal risk is concentration, not merely whether SPCX is a good company.** Employment, future compensation, and a large asset would all depend on the same company and AI thesis.
4. **Selling and diversifying vested shares is the cleanest control** if the award is accepted and trading-policy windows permit it.

## 4. Bull case

1. **Connectivity is a scaled, profitable foundation.** Q2 Connectivity revenue was $4.291B, segment operating income was $1.656B, and adjusted EBITDA was $2.597B. Starlink reached 12.0M subscribers, double the prior year, while enterprise and government revenue rose 108% year over year. [S5]
2. **Growth is already visible, not purely conceptual.** Consolidated Q2 revenue grew 92% and adjusted EBITDA 191%. AI produced $2.561B of Q2 revenue and positive segment adjusted EBITDA, even though it remained GAAP operating-loss-making. [S5]
3. **Vertical integration creates a unique stack.** Launch, satellites, connectivity, terrestrial compute, models, and Cursor’s enterprise developer workflow can reinforce one another. SpaceX can internalize launches and connectivity that competitors must buy.
4. **Starship can change several cost curves at once.** Rapid reuse and higher payload capacity would improve Starlink deployment, launch economics, government missions, and any orbital-compute plan. This is why launch itself can be only $8 of Morgan Stanley’s SOTP while still being the essential enabler.
5. **Compute demand and contracts provide near-term evidence.** Management reported $14.1B of contracted cloud sales and $1.6B of incremental Q2 AI-infrastructure revenue from those arrangements. [S5]
6. **The balance sheet buys execution time.** About $100B of cash and marketable securities offsets near-term funding risk, even though it does not fund the entire long-range plan. [S4][S5]
7. **Cursor can make AI monetization less abstract.** The August 14 all-stock closing brought an enterprise product, developer distribution, and usage data into SpaceX’s AI segment. The legal closing and award conversion are confirmed in the 8-K, not merely announced in press coverage. [S6]
8. **Street estimates leave substantial upside.** The cleanest 36-analyst set has a $220 median; Goldman is at $220, Bernstein at $248, Oppenheimer at $280, and Morgan Stanley at $300. [S7]

## 5. Bear case

1. **Excellent operations can still be a bad entry price.** A $2.073T market cap against $23.04B of trailing revenue is about 90× sales. Even rapid growth can coincide with multiple compression.
2. **The market is capitalizing unproven outcomes today.** Fully reusable high-cadence Starship and cost-competitive orbital computing are engineering and regulatory projects, not established businesses. Morningstar assigns only 7% to its “Moonshot” scenario and values SPCX at $63. [S13]
3. **Free cash flow is deeply negative.** The standardized trailing figure is -$32.52B. First-half purchases of property and equipment were $28.476B, versus $6.965B a year earlier. [S1][S4]
4. **Financing can dilute shareholders.** Morgan Stanley’s model reportedly remains FCF-negative until 2035 and needs about $84B per year of external capital from 2027–2034. If debt capacity is insufficient, equity issuance or slower deployment follows. [S11]
5. **Cursor created immediate and contingent dilution.** Closing shares added about 3.0% to the pre-deal basic count. Assumed Cursor RSUs and options add another potential 0.54% before considering ordinary SpaceX grants. [S6]
6. **Lockup supply remains material.** Eligibility is not the same as selling, but hundreds of millions of additional shares can become tradeable on individual release dates, increasing two-sided volatility.
7. **AI demand may be cyclical or less profitable than modeled.** Neocloud revenue can scale quickly while requiring enormous capex and facing customer concentration, chip supply, power, pricing, and model-commoditization risk.
8. **Starlink subscriber growth does not eliminate unit-economics risk.** Q2 ARPU was $66 versus $85 a year earlier. Scale and enterprise mix currently offset the decline, but that tradeoff can reverse. [S5]
9. **Governance is concentrated.** The dual-class structure leaves Elon Musk with voting control. Capital allocation can favor long-duration projects that public shareholders would not independently choose. [S3]
10. **The observed drawdown is a warning against normal-volatility assumptions.** The stock moved from $225.64 to $104.83 and back above $150 in one quarter. A 50% endpoint loss is not a “six sigma” event for this security.

### Explicit probability of a 50% loss in 24 months

- Threshold: **$76.36**.
- Central endpoint estimate: **20%**.
- Reasonable subjective range: **15%–30%**.
- Probability of touching $76.36 at any point during the next 24 months: **higher than 20%, but not estimated here**.

Why 20% rather than a token tail probability:

- the $75 Morgan Stanley bear case and $63 Morningstar fair value both cross the threshold;
- the stock has already completed a greater peak-to-trough move;
- valuation and financing expose it to both fundamental disappointment and multiple compression.

Why not 40% or more:

- the company has exceptional current growth, a profitable Connectivity segment, and a large liquidity buffer;
- the stock is already 32% below its high;
- the broader Street is bullish, though analyst consensus is not independent evidence when models share assumptions.

No reliable two-year option-implied distribution was found in the reviewed sources. Presenting a more precise probability would be fabricated confidence.

## 7. What the scenarios mean for an $8.5M RSU offer

Assume, only for illustration, that $8.5M is converted at exactly $153 into a fixed **55,556-unit** grant and that there are no forfeitures. Actual offer documents may use a trailing average, board-approval-date price, or another convention.

| 24-month case | SPCX price | Mark-to-market value of all 55,556 units | Value of 50% of units |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $63 | **$3.50M** | **$1.75M** |
| Base | $240 | **$13.33M** | **$6.67M** |
| Bull | $450 | **$25.00M** | **$12.50M** |
| Probability-weighted | $257.10 | **$14.28M** | **$7.14M** |
| Exact 50%-down threshold | $76.36 | **$4.24M** | **$2.12M** |

“Value of 50% of units” is a simple two-year straight-line proxy. It is **not** expected take-home pay: units may vest monthly, quarterly, or annually; vesting dates have different stock prices; withholding reduces shares; vested shares may be sold earlier; and leaving can forfeit unvested units.

The more useful personal framing is:

- **Downside planning value:** $3.5M–$6.4M for the full nominal grant at $63–$115, before vesting and tax.
- **Base planning value:** about $12.2M–$13.3M at $220–$240.
- **Upside is real but should not finance fixed obligations.**

## 8. Dilution, lockups, and new-hire liquidity

### Cursor acquisition dilution

The August 14 8-K says:

- 389,289,254 Class A shares went to Cursor common and preferred holders;
- 1,752,426 Class A shares covered vested Cursor RSUs before tax withholding;
- 29,128,326 SpaceX RSUs replaced unvested Cursor RSUs;
- 44,365,047 SpaceX options replaced Cursor options.

The first two items increased basic shares by **391.042M**, or **2.97% of the pre-close count**. If every assumed RSU settled and every option were exercised, the additional 73.493M shares would equal another **0.54% of the current pro forma basic count**. Options should not be treated as certain dilution without exercise prices and vesting.

SpaceX also granted 25.918M RSUs under its 2024 Plan between April 1 and June 12. The 10-Q does not make that a steady quarterly run rate, but it confirms continuing employee issuance. [S4]

Elon Musk’s holdings are subject to a separate approximately one-year restriction extending to June 2027. Exact eligibility varies by holder, affiliate status, award, and waiver. **An unlock means shares may be sold, not that they will be sold.** [S14][S15]

### What is known—and not known—about a new Cursor hire

The August 14 8-K describes legacy Cursor awards converted at closing. It **does not establish the terms, registration status, or liquidity of a post-closing new-hire grant**. A new hire should not assume the acquisition conversion terms apply.

SpaceX’s August 4 Form S-8 and reoffer prospectus registered 136.950M employee-plan shares and confirms that employee shares can be registered for public resale, but it also says sales remain subject to applicable lockups and market-standoff agreements. Registration alone does not create an always-open trading window. [S16]

Before valuing the offer, obtain written answers to:

1. Exact number of SPCX units, not only a dollar “face value.”
2. Price or averaging period used to convert $8.5M into units.
3. Grant-approval date, vesting commencement date, cliff, and monthly/quarterly cadence.
4. Whether vested RSUs settle immediately into Class A shares.
5. Which equity plan covers the grant and whether the underlying shares are S-8 registered.
6. Any lockup, market-standoff, blackout, minimum-holding, or pre-clearance rule applicable to the grant.
7. Normal employee trading windows and whether a 10b5-1 plan is available.
8. Tax-withholding method and rate: net settlement, sell-to-cover, or cash payment.
9. Treatment at termination, leave, acquisition, role change, or disability.
10. Refresh-grant policy and whether the headline grant is intended to replace future refreshes.

For a U.S. employee, RSU settlement is generally W-2 ordinary income at fair market value, followed by capital gain or loss on later price movement. State residence, timing, and the award documents can materially change cash needs; use a qualified tax adviser before relying on the net value.

## 9. Bottom line for the Cursor hire

**Accepting the equity risk can be rational; depending on the headline value cannot.**

The operating company is stronger than a simple “space IPO” label suggests, but the stock price already assumes a meaningful portion of the AI and Starship future. SPCX can become a far more valuable company while the stock still falls if results merely fail to outrun the valuation.

A practical decision rule:

- Take the role if the work, team, cash compensation, and career trajectory are attractive **even when the equity is underwritten at $63–$75**.
- Treat approximately **$4.2M**, not $8.5M, as the stress value at the reader’s feared 50% decline, before vesting and tax.
- Do not count unvested RSUs toward near-term financial independence.
- If permitted, diversify vested shares on a predetermined schedule rather than making a fresh all-in SPCX decision every trading window.
- Negotiate on **unit count, conversion formula, vesting start, and liquidity language**; those can matter as much as another increment of face value.

The report’s 24-month expected value is positive, but expected value is not certainty. For this reader, the relevant question is not “Is SPCX likely to rise?” It is: **“Would I still be glad I took the job if the full grant marks at $3.5M–$4.2M and only half has vested?”** If yes, the upside is compelling. If no, the package is too dependent on one volatile security.

## 10. Key uncertainties and limitations

1. **Share count:** 13.573B is a transparent pro forma estimate, not a reported September period-end count.
2. **Dilution:** converted Cursor options lack public exercise-price detail in the closing 8-K; future grants, taxes, buybacks, and financing can change the denominator.
3. **Enterprise value:** the simplified scenarios hold $60.3B net cash constant despite likely cash burn and financing.
4. **Analyst data:** aggregators differ materially. StockAnalysis excludes $75 and $800 outliers from its displayed 36-analyst range while other services include them.
5. **Morgan Stanley detail:** the full research note is not publicly accessible. Segment values and long-range financing assumptions come from dated secondary summaries.
6. **Lockups:** published quantities are maximum shares becoming eligible, not actual sell orders. Holder-specific agreements control.
7. **Cursor financials:** the Q2 filing predated closing, and the August 14 8-K did not include a completed purchase-price allocation or pro forma combined financial statements.
8. **Compensation:** the actual offer letter and plan documents were not reviewed. The $8.5M conversion and straight-line vesting examples are illustrative.
9. **Probability model:** only three discrete endpoints are used. Real returns are continuous, path-dependent, and can exceed both tails.
10. **Date:** this report is dated on a non-trading Sunday and uses the September 18 close.
